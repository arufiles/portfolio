Ngajum Geophysical Survey Project

Sae L'Sima, Ngajum, Malang, East Java · Research Team / Field Assistant · 2025-2026

A lecturer-led project to find groundwater sources across multiple plots of land. Over six field visits, the team first tried to trace a nearby river to its source, then used SP, resistivity, and PQWT surveys to locate and confirm a possible groundwater source.

  • Measured land plots across multiple field visits using a Garmin GPS device.
  • Participated in river tracing activities to investigate potential water sources, although access limitations prevented the team from reaching the river's origin.
  • Performed Self-Potential (SP) measurements at a location suspected by local stakeholders to contain a groundwater source, followed by electrical resistivity measurements along an extended survey line for further investigation.
  • Performed PQWT measurements at a separate site within the same area during the initial drilling phase and documented field activities throughout the exploration and drilling process.
Electrical resistivity SP PQWT Garmin GPS
Result: Surveyed 19 plots covering a total area of 38,454.49 m² (approximately 3.85 hectares). Drilling commenced during the final field visit, informed by the findings of previous surveys.

Research & data analysis

GPR Investigation, Wlingi Dam

Wlingi Da, Blitar, East Java · Research Team / Field Assistant · 2024

This project involved Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR) surveys across multiple areas of Wlingi Dam to investigate potential water seepage and identify subsurface anomalies. The findings from the slope survey were further developed into my undergraduate thesis.

  • Collected GPR data across multiple survey areas using a 90 MHz antenna.
  • Processed radargrams using several filtering techniques to improve data readability.
  • Interpreted B-scan profiles to identify and evaluate subsurface anomalies.
  • Analyzed anomalous responses to assess potential subsurface material variations and voids that could be associated with seepage.
GPR GeoScan32 GPRNet

Result: Identified several anomalous zones potentially associated with subsurface material variations or voids. However, the GPR findings alone did not confirm active water seepage.
